# Intercom Fin — prospect list for the "cheaper / open-source Fin" test

Compiled 2026-10-17 from web search. Vendor case studies are self-reported; verify before outreach.

**Riskiest assumption being tested:** small companies paying for Fin would switch to a cheaper (open-source / self-hostable) AI support agent if its resolution rate is within a few points of Fin's.

**Test:** offer to run our agent for free on ~200 of their past tickets, compare resolution rate against Fin's, then quote a price.

These come from Intercom/Fin's own customer stories, so they are *satisfied, public reference customers* — the hardest group to switch. Useful for learning their Job and success criteria; low odds of a sale.

## Complaints about the bill (anonymous; no company named)

- Reddit: "Why my Intercom bill jumped from $4k to $9k/month" (via secondhand summaries)
- A user's bill went from $119 → $854/month after moving to per-resolution pricing
- G2 reviewer: started at $200/month, hit $1,400 during a product launch
- r/SaaS: "billing shot up by 120%"
- Audit story: 16 conversations billed as resolutions, 9 of them not actually resolved by Fin
- Main complaint: "assumed resolutions" (customer goes quiet → billed $0.99)

Original threads could not be opened from the research sandbox. Open them directly and DM the authors. They are the real price-first segment.

## Findings that change the plan

1. **"Cheaper Fin" is already crowded with closed products:** My AskAI (~$0.10/ticket), Macha, YourGPT, Zipchat, Gleap, Supportson, Varley, Aimdoc, Omago, Corebee, and more. Most of the "Fin is too expensive" content online is *their* SEO. Open source is the only unclaimed angle; price alone is not.
2. **Trigger event:** Intercom rebranded to Fin (May 2026), and Salesforce agreed to acquire it (~$3.6B, reported closed 10 Sep 2026; one source says still pending). Acquisition-driven roadmap and pricing anxiety is a natural switching trigger worth asking about.
3. **Billing trust, not just price:** the "assumed resolution" rule means customers can't verify what they pay for. A verifiable-resolution billing model (or a flat self-hosted cost) may land harder than a lower per-ticket number.
4. **Quality depends on the knowledge base:** an independent test found ~38% average resolution on small businesses (28–31% with sparse FAQs, 47–52% with good docs). Any comparison test must use the prospect's own help center.

## Better ways to find price-first prospects

- Open the Reddit / G2 / Capterra complaint threads above and contact the authors directly.
- Tech-lookup tools (BuiltWith, TechnologyChecker.io) list sites running the Intercom widget. Filter to companies with 10–100 employees.
- Fin's Early Stage Program alumni (like Debbie) get a discount that later expires. Reach them around when that discount runs out.
